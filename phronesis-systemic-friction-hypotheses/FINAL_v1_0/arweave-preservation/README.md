## Scope

This paper tests a specific inference: whether fixed-policy self-penalty is sufficient evidence that an adapting optimizer will settle on restraint. In the stipulated synthetic model, constant maximal manipulation becomes self-penalizing while an adaptive manipulate-and-recover policy remains profitable. The result is therefore a counterexample to that inference pattern within the model; it is not a claim that adaptive optimizers generally resist restraint or that systemic friction in general cannot support cooperation.

The paper is descriptive and non-authoritative. Physical-exposure and deception-maintenance mechanisms remain empirical conjectures. Independent specialist peer review has not been obtained, empirical validation has not been performed, and proposed nonlinear, belief-state, and defensive-architecture extensions remain future work.
