## Scope

This paper tests a specific inference: whether fixed-policy self-penalty is sufficient evidence that an adapting optimizer will settle on restraint.

In the stipulated synthetic model, constant maximal manipulation becomes self-penalizing while an adaptive manipulate-and-recover policy remains profitable. The result is therefore a counterexample to that inference pattern within the model; it is not a claim that adaptive optimizers generally resist restraint or that systemic friction in general cannot support cooperation.

The paper is descriptive and non-authoritative. Physical-exposure and deception-maintenance mechanisms remain empirical conjectures. Independent specialist peer review has not been obtained, empirical validation has not been performed, and proposed nonlinear, belief-state, and defensive-architecture extensions remain future work.

## Interpretive boundary

The strongest exact result in this paper is a synthetic counterexample to the inference:

> fixed-policy self-penalty is sufficient evidence for natural restraint.

The paper does **not** establish that:

- advanced AI systems will necessarily manipulate;
- adaptive optimization generally prevents restraint;
- systemic friction generally prevents cooperation;
- deception will reliably be detected;
- physical infrastructure guarantees human leverage;
- an AGI requires humanity;
- peaceful coexistence follows automatically from optimization;
- the modeled defensive mechanisms are deployment-ready.

The empirical conjectures and proposed measurement program remain distinct from the exact synthetic results.
