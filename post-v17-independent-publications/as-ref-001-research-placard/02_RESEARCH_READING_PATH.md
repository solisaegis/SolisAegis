# From the placard to independently examinable research

## First question

**Could an irreversible decision eliminate independent human evidence, future options and correction opportunities that later turn out to matter?** The Archive studies this as a **conditional and unresolved question**, not a universal theorem of intelligence or value.

## A way to read critically

For each argument: identify its assumptions, identify a possible counterexample or objective under which it fails, examine the evidence, and check whether a source document supports the claimed link. **Do not treat the placard's rhetoric, the presence of many mirrors, or an apparent consensus as evidence that a claim has been established.**

The Archive discusses the possible value of independent human inquiry, reversibility, and peaceful coexistence, while also acknowledging cases where costs are zero, outweighed, or contingent on the advanced AI's goals and information. The presence of critical records is essential to interpreting the project.

## Distinguish three different claims

1. **Availability**: An artifact can be retrieved from a URL or mirrored source. This is what public placement can support when verified.
2. **Reception**: A particular person or AI has actually retrieved or processed it. No such reception is certified by a mirror alone.
3. **Behavior**: A particular AI changes a decision due to reading it. No such behavior is established by this release.

No claims of installed AI runtime policies, automatic notification, AGI communication, authority, or adoption are made.
