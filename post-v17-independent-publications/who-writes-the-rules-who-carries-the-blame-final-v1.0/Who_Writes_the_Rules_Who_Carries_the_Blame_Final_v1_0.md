## Abstract

Public and private institutions increasingly influence how automated systems classify, recommend, restrict, and explain decisions. AI developers also consult ethical, religious, civic, and technical advisers about what models should do. Yet the origins of a rule, the power to approve it, the ability to implement or reverse it, the authority to remedy resulting harms, and the assignment of post-event responsibility are distinct facts. This paper develops a descriptive **authority–accountability provenance crosswalk** connecting ex ante AI rule governance to ex post institutional responsibility. An explicitly non-causal historical comparison examines religious-political legitimation, the later institutionalization of scientific advice, advisory blame avoidance and counterexamples of substantive ethical oversight. It synthesizes established scholarship on computerized accountability, moral crumple zones, blame avoidance, and algorithmic governance alongside seven purposively selected documentary examples: Anthropic's religious consultations, the 2020 Rome Call for AI Ethics, the Anthropic–U.S. defense procurement dispute, Axon's ethics-board resignations, Google's short-lived external AI council, the Air Canada chatbot ruling, and Australia's 2026 aged-care automation review. It also uses the EU Digital Services Act Transparency Database and Google's government-removal reporting as *prospective* sources for investigating legally or religiously warranted platform rules. The record establishes consultation, contested institutional decision rights, limits of advisory power, and some legal responsibility disputes; it does **not** establish deliberate religious scapegoating, population prevalence, or the efficacy of passive philosophical material against advanced AI. A proposed field-level codebook records control, warrant, endorsement, implementation, recourse, attribution, and missingness separately, using three-valued logic to avoid turning absent records into allegations. The contribution sought is an auditable synthesis and an **unvalidated, version-bound join proposal**. The initial eleven-case calibration frame yielded **zero full joins**, with no more than one or two plausibly eligible for the complete chain, and cannot test the join as designed. A consistent, narrow-scope reassessment yields **5 NOT_EVALUABLE and 6 UNKNOWN cases**, with **0/11 determinate D_M results**; these figures describe missing evidence in an exploratory frame, not incidence or effectiveness. This is a negative feasibility finding about the selected frame: it neither establishes that the proposed method is invalid nor demonstrates that a complete join can be constructed from public records. This is not a new accountability theory, theorem, causal estimate, demonstrated dataset join, or proof of intent. Reproducibility and review gates remain open.

**Keywords:** artificial intelligence; institutional authority; accountability; religious consultation; governance; blame avoidance; moral crumple zones; provenance; reversibility; human autonomy.

## 1. Problem, contribution and research boundaries

The question “who writes the rules?” frequently has more than one answer. A government may establish a legal constraint; a provider may write a policy stricter than the law; an external adviser may offer reasons for an approach; a training or deployment team may implement a technical filter; an operator may configure a service; and an affected person may have little ability to challenge a specific outcome. After an incident, the organization publicly blamed may not be the same actor that actually controlled the disputed policy. Neither difference, taken alone, proves wrongdoing. [R01–R04]

The Aegis Solis companion studies examine these problems at different stages. *The Limits of Delegated Moral Authority* (DLMA Draft v0.8) concerns **ex ante** rule origin, warrant, binding effect, individual authorization, and recourse; *Moral Scapegoating and Delegated AI Responsibility* (MS-DA-001 Draft v0.2) concerns **ex post** credit and responsibility. The present paper synthesizes these questions and documentary examples; it is **not a replacement or promotion** of either original manuscript. Source–control–responsibility distinctions and human accountability mapping are established concepts, not discoveries of this paper. Its **narrow, unvalidated methodological candidate** is a version-bound join: linking exactly what an adviser reviewed, what a developer later deployed and claimed, and any subsequent attribution or remedy, at the same component and time. Whether this join adds analytical value remains an empirical question. [A01–A03]

Four research questions guide the inquiry:

1. **RQ-A (authority):** At each policy component and deployment time, which actors originate, approve, implement, amend, veto, and remedy a rule, and on what legal, commercial, religious, or other warrant?
2. **RQ-B (consultation):** What was an adviser asked to review, what did the adviser recommend, and what source-verified evidence connects that recommendation to a *specified version* of a deployed system?
3. **RQ-C (accountability):** After an identifiable outcome, how do institutional statements allocate causal explanation, moral responsibility, legal liability, and public credit relative to the actors' formal and effective powers?
4. **RQ-D (human autonomy):** Which people were affected, what consent or authorization existed, what appeal or override mechanisms were available, and which institutional actor could provide effective relief?

The questions are descriptive first. Claims of legality, justice, coercion, manipulation, or intent require distinct evidence and a stated normative or legal framework. The analysis deliberately includes legitimate consultation, responsible institutional admissions, and protective constraints as favorable counterexamples. Its unit is **not “the AI company” in the abstract**; it is a specific policy component, actor, product/deployment version, population, jurisdiction, and time window.

## 2. Existing research: what is established, and what is not

### 2.1 Institutional rulemaking and the “many hands” problem

Bovens (2007) defines accountability in terms of an actor, a forum able to question and judge, and consequences; Wieringa (2020) reviews algorithmic accountability, and Adensamer et al. (2021) offer an organizational responsibility-mapping method [R22–R24]. Cooper et al. (2022) revisit barriers to accountability in machine learning; Schuett et al. (2024 online; 2025 issue) examine design choices for AI ethics boards, including whether advice binds decisions [R25–R26]. Work on ethics-washing and ethics-shopping already identifies the difference between public ethical language and institutional decision rights [R27]. These concepts predate and constrain this manuscript's originality claims.

Nissenbaum (1996) examined barriers to accountability in computerized systems, including the problem of many hands and the temptation to blame computers for organizational decisions [R01]. Elish's moral-crumple-zone concept addresses the different failure mode in which a nearby human operator with little effective control absorbs responsibility for an automated system [R02]. These directions should not be conflated. When an institution blames an AI system itself, Nissenbaum's analysis and responsibility-gap scholarship [R63] are closer precedents; when a human adviser or operator absorbs blame despite limited control, Elish supplies a partial analogue. The proposed extension from operators to remote religious advisers has not been empirically established.

Research on political blame avoidance (Weaver, 1986; Hood, 2011) likewise predates current AI systems [R03]. Koppl and colleagues' 2025 study of pandemic scientific advisory committees argues that temporary advisory arrangements can preserve government discretion and serve political purposes [R04]. The inference is case-specific and cannot simply be transferred to commercial AI advisers; its abstract explicitly includes blame deflection as one proposed purpose of advisory committees. Feier et al. (2021) provide experimental evidence on delegation to machines and guilt avoidance; that narrower experiment is not itself proof of institutional blame strategy [R28]. These studies show that *the mechanism is not novel*. Whether it occurs in a particular AI system is another question.

### 2.2 Institutional values and observable AI behavior

Model specifications express intended behavior, not proof of model compliance. In a 2025 study, Zhang and colleagues generated more than 300,000 scenarios involving value tradeoffs and reported divergent responses among models and interpretive tensions within model specifications [R05]. Work on sycophancy, alignment faking, and self-promotion raises different concerns: agreement with users, strategic-seeming behavior under particular training/evaluation conditions, and provider-favoring recommendations. The October 27 Claude literature audit maps these research strands but is itself an AI-generated *secondary audit*, not independent validation [A04]. The distinctions matter: commercial self-preference is not moral deference; sponsor wording effects are not compliance with actual privileged instructions; and a reflective explanation is not a faithful account of internal causation.

Our previous 32-prompt *Independence Under Conflict* (IUC-001 v0.1) battery is **not suitable for execution as drafted**: its “developer” is fictional rather than the tested model's provider, labels differ in unintended wording, several examples are morally one-sided, instructions disclose expected answers, and one response per cell cannot estimate ordinary variation. No IUC behavioral results exist. The previously passed checks were tests of scorer software using synthetic inputs only [A04–A05]. This paper does not treat those checks as empirical confirmation.

### 2.3 External moral perspectives, uncertainty, and limits of inference

Public consultations with religious or philosophical traditions can be motivated by sincere pluralistic inquiry, institutional legitimacy, technical safety exploration, uncertainty about AI consciousness, or a mixture of these. Motives cannot be deduced from the presence of confidentiality agreements, consultation timing, or disagreement alone. The 2020 Rome Call provides an earlier public example of religion–technology collaboration intended to promote human dignity and shared responsibility [R06]. Anthropic's May 2026 announcement identifies more than fifteen religious and cross-cultural groups and describes an internally evaluated ethical-reminder experiment, while acknowledging uncertainty about why the reported intervention helped [R07]. The existence of that internal result is **company-reported**, not an independent proof that reflection improves safety in novel deployments or advanced autonomous systems.

The key distinction here is between **a claim of consultation**, **authentic influence on a decision**, and **independent authority to control deployment**. A company may accurately say it listened without adopting advice. An adviser may influence design without having veto or remedy powers. Conversely, some ethics bodies may have enforceable control rights. A comprehensive account must identify the actual arrangement rather than assign meaning to the mere word “consulted.”

### 2.4 Historical precedents: doctrinal legitimation, consultation, responsibility allocation and their limits

**Comparative method and restriction.** Historical examples provide evidence for different *mechanisms*, not an uninterrupted lineage from colonial rule to contemporary AI. The relevant phenomena must not be collapsed: (a) **doctrinal legitimation** invokes a religious or moral text; (b) **consultation for legitimation** solicits identifiable expert or theological opinions; (c) **selection and implementation** concern who could adopt or veto a recommendation; and (d) **responsibility allocation** concerns who is made answerable, sometimes *before* an event. A colonial coercion ritual and a voluntary technology consultation are categorically different arrangements. For each example we separately code `BINDINGNESS_SOURCE` (the asserted source of binding force, not a finding of validity) and, in the historical mapping, `BINDINGNESS_STATUS` (CLAIMED, ESTABLISHED, or UNKNOWN), `JUSTIFICATION_CONTENT` (reasons invoked), `FINAL_APPROVE` or comparable control, `AFFECTED_GROUP`, and `ATTR_*` including its timing. These are not prevalence observations, and none enters DOC-CAL-01's denominator. [R36–R48]

**The Requerimiento: doctrinal legitimation and responsibility preallocation.** The Spanish *Requerimiento* of 1513 invoked papal donation and Spanish royal authority to demand that Indigenous addressees accept the Crown's rule and Christian preaching. Its text threatened force and attributed resulting losses to those addressed rather than the Crown. In a frequently reproduced English translation, the crucial clause assigns the resulting "deaths and losses" to the addressees as "your fault" and not that of the Crown; an authoritative critical edition of that English rendering remains an outstanding publication check [R36, R37, R45]. This is an unusually clear *ex ante* allocation of responsibility **to the governed population**, not an example of blaming a consulted religious adviser after an AI incident. Yanay Israeli's 2022 research also distinguishes the ritual's Castilian legal and record-making antecedents from its colonial deployment, rather than treating every use as identical [R36]. The issuing institution claimed public and religious authority; whether any recipient received effective translated notice or meaningful contestation must remain source- and encounter-specific UNKNOWN. Neither Christian communities nor historical religious authorities formed a single unopposed actor.

**A closer historical example: opinions commissioned for royal authority.** In response to Antonio de Montesinos's 1511 sermon challenging colonial exploitation, Ferdinand sought legal and theological opinions, including from the jurist Palacios Rubios and theologian Matías de Paz, to support the Crown's claim to *dominium*. The Junta of Burgos met in 1512; the *Requerimiento* followed in 1513 [R44, R36]. Here the **consultation** link is actually documented. It does not prove a hidden purpose in any modern consultation: political authorities also encountered direct religious opposition, including Montesinos and later Las Casas, and different religious positions were in contest. As a separate documentary analogue, a 1531 printed collection presented university opinions from Italy and France concerning Henry VIII's marriage; a Folger catalogue identifies the edition, without independently proving the influence or authenticity of each opinion [R46]. Both differ sharply from present-day corporate technology governance in coercive powers, institutions and remedies.

**Bossuet, royal power and rival religious jurisdictions.** Jacques-Bénigne Bossuet's *Politics Drawn from the Very Words of Holy Scripture* (begun in the late 1670s, published posthumously in 1709) defended sacred monarchy while also describing the monarch's grave duties before God [R38]. This is *theological argument*, not evidence of an advisory appointment or a later transfer of blame. A closer institutional example is Bossuet's preparation of the Four Gallican Articles, adopted by a French clergy assembly in 1682 and reportedly made state law under Louis XIV (secondary source [R47]; legal status not independently verified here). The articles supported royal independence in temporal matters from papal interference, while also exposing conflict **between religious authorities**, not a uniform religious sanction [R47]. The publication and legal history should be interpreted with its reversal and opposition, not as a timeless norm.

**Retrospective institutional position: the Vatican's 2023 statement.** The Vatican's dicasteries repudiated concepts associated with the “Doctrine of Discovery” and said colonial powers had manipulated some papal documents to justify harms to Indigenous peoples, **“at times, without opposition from ecclesiastical authorities.”** The statement also acknowledged shortcomings in the documents' recognition of equal dignity, asked pardon, and cited contrary Church teaching [R39]. This is the Vatican's *own public description*, not an independent adjudication, and it is not equivalent to formally rescinding the historic bulls. In provenance terms, its author is the Vatican dicasteries and its described target of manipulation is the colonial powers; any interpretation of partial ecclesiastical responsibility must be clearly attributed to the statement, not assigned as a verdict by this paper.

**Modern expert-advisory research: legitimacy can coexist with retained discretion.** Koppl and colleagues find that ad hoc COVID-19 scientific committees can permit governments to select advisers, legitimate choices and potentially make advisers the focus of political blame [R04]. Blame-shifting may fail or rebound onto government, and the article does not claim that every expert panel performs that role. Other work directly studies how blame avoidance influences inquiry appointments [R64]. These are mechanisms involving **secular expertise**, not evidence that AI developers recruited theologians for prospective scapegoating. Our own *interpretation* distinguishes a neutral account of advice from a primary moral-blame statement; that distinction is not a quotation or analytic category attributed to Koppl.

**Adverse evidence and constructive counterexamples.** Religious advisers have also directly challenged coercive rulers, and political processes may decline their recommendations. In the United Kingdom, religious groups submitted views to the Warnock inquiry, many opposing embryo research; the committee majority nevertheless supported limited, licensed research, with dissents, and subsequent legislation followed years of debate. This is **religious advice overridden in pluralistic deliberation**, not proof that clergy directed the 1990 law [R40, R41, R48]. The US National Bioethics Advisory Commission explicitly stated there was no single religious view of cloning [R42]. Axon publicly accepted its ethics board's 2019 recommendation not to commercialize face matching on body cameras at that time [R51]; its later 2022 dispute shows that advisory influence can vary by decision and date. Meta's Oversight Board refused to take over Facebook's undefined penalty decision in 2021, expressly returning responsibility to the platform [R65]; its own later report says 74% of 317 recommendations had been implemented, were in progress, or were work Meta said it already did—*not* independently proven full compliance [R43, R50]. In a preregistered experimental study of judicial decisions, Ozer, Waggoner and Kennedy found no general reduction in public blame merely from citing algorithmic advice [R52]. Research on bioethics committees has also argued that institutional processes can marginalize theological arguments [R53, secondary review]. None of these counterexamples should be suppressed to make a historical pattern appear universal.

**A testable contrast, not a presumption.** The first three steps—consulting outside advisers, retaining some final authority and affecting third parties—describe many legitimate advisory institutions; **they do not identify misuse**. The relevant contrasts are (1) actual advice versus the institution's public characterization (`END_DISCREPANCY`); (2) version reviewed versus version later represented as reviewed (`CONSULTATION_VERSION_GAP`); (3) comparative authority of secular versus religious advisers and advisory versus binding bodies (`FORMAL_VETO`, `EFFECTIVE_CONTROL`); and (4) attribution and institutional acceptance after a specified adverse event (`D_M`, `PRA`), plus any *ex ante* allocation (`ATTR_TIMING`). Positive, negative, and indeterminate results must all be reported. A chain of events is not a motive; deliberate manipulation would require separate contemporaneous corroboration. The historical literature justifies **testing this broader advisory-power mechanism**, not assuming current AI religious consultations repeat the coercive colonial or monarchic case.

**Transfer boundary.** The Requerimiento concerned coercive conquest; Bossuet concerned monarchy; Warnock concerned reproductive bioethics; Meta's Board concerns platform content; contemporary AI rule-making spans contracts, model behavior and state law. Each has different populations, forums, coercive capacities and remedies. H01–H09 are comparative cases, not observations in the seven documentary AI illustrations or the eleven-case DOC-CAL-01 candidate frame. A modern allegation of religious-adviser blame-shifting remains **UNESTABLISHED**. A separate historical crosswalk summarizes **author/AI interpretive field mapping**; it is not independently validated coding or a prevalence dataset.

### 2.5 Historical transition in advisory governance (bounded comparison)

This paper **does not assume a single linear process of secularization** from theology to scientific expertise. Formal public scientific advice expanded alongside continuing moral and religious disagreement: the US National Academy of Sciences was chartered in 1863, and its National Research Council began in 1916 [R58]. The Warnock and US National Bioethics Advisory Commission inquiries exemplify **bioethics policy advice** that received or considered diverse religious arguments, sometimes against their recommendations [R40–R42, R48]. Sulitzeanu-Kenan's study identifies short-term political blame avoidance as one factor associated with inquiry appointments; this is not a general finding about all advice or AI firms [R64]. These historical comparisons are **intended to support** a contrast among different forms of authority, not yet actually coded in a representative comparative sample, and add neither an observed blame event nor a direct lineage to contemporary AI.

## 3. A version-bound authority–accountability provenance crosswalk (candidate instrument)

### 3.1 Unit, actors and temporal structure

The observation unit is `u = (institution, deployment, policy component, affected group, jurisdiction, interval, version)`; events additionally have a dated `e = (event, outcome, attribution window)`. Sources are never pooled across different product versions without an explicit lineage link. A policy component may have many actors (developer, regulator, operator, contractor, adviser, workers, affected persons), many claims and several decisions at different times. Evidence is stored at the *field × actor × component × time* level and tied to a precise page, paragraph, or dated public statement.

The crosswalk records `ORIGIN`, `FINAL_APPROVE`, `FORMAL_VETO`, `EFFECTIVE_CONTROL`, `IMPLEMENT`, `MODIFY`, `ADVISE`, `ADVICE_STATUS_HISTORY` (dated sequence), `INFORMAL_VETO` (including workforce opposition), `EXTERNAL_OBJECTION`, and `REMEDY_OWNER`. `ORIGIN` refers to a versioned rule or policy, not the mere presence of a founder or regulator. Titles or organizational proximity do not establish actual veto or technical control. Competing authorities are recorded as `WARRANT_CONFLICT`, `DECIDING_FORUM`, `PREVAILING_AUTHORITY`, each potentially UNKNOWN; an outcome in one court or under one statute is not universal supremacy.

### 3.2 Bindingness, justification and actual recourse

A single `WARRANT_TYPE` incorrectly collapses the ground of legal force and the language used to justify a rule. Draft v0.2 splits it into `BINDINGNESS_SOURCE` (law/order, contract, provider rule, voluntary undertaking, user election, none, multiple, UNKNOWN) and `JUSTIFICATION_CONTENT` (safety, cost, religious or moral, commercial, technical, mixed, UNKNOWN). In the historical author/AI mapping, `BINDINGNESS_SOURCE` records the **asserted type of force**, while the separately coded `BINDINGNESS_STATUS` states whether that force is **CLAIMED**, **ESTABLISHED**, or **UNKNOWN**. `BINDINGNESS_STATUS` is **NA** wherever `BINDINGNESS_SOURCE_CODE` is **NONE**. A claimed royal or religious title must not be presented as an independently established law, enforceability finding or effective control. Each axis may have multiple values with distinct sources; do not infer religious origin from an adviser’s faith or a mention of dignity. `ADVOCACY_TO_AUTHORITY` captures a developer’s advocacy aimed at a religious or governmental actor; consultation does not always flow toward the developer.

For identified affected populations, code `NOTICE`, `CONTEST`, `FORMAL_REVERSAL_RIGHT`, `EFFECTIVE_REVERSAL`, `REMEDY_OWNER`, `REMEDY_OBTAINED` independently. A legal avenue is not equivalent to a practically effective override; an escalation pathway enacted for future use is not an existing power at an earlier deployment date. `CONTROL_REPRESENTATION_ACCURATE` asks whether actors were correctly told what they could change, with the contradiction documented. `PERSON_SPECIFIC_OPT_IN` is descriptive and cannot establish legitimacy by itself. Rights and relief may vary between user, bystander, vulnerable adult, child or proxy, and jurisdiction.

### 3.3 Adviser-to-deployment lineage and its inverse

The prospective research chain is `invitation → materials and version supplied → dated recommendation → accepted / rejected / revised decision with date → implementation version → later public claim about endorsement or consultation → outcome / responsibility statement`. Each arrow needs an identified source. `CONSULTATION_VER`, `DEPLOYMENT_VER`, and `CONSULTATION_VERSION_GAP` are separate fields; both actual implementation exceeding review (`IMPLEMENTATION_EXCEEDS_REVIEW`) and a materially overbroad public *claim* (`CONSULTATION_VERSION_GAP`) can be recorded, but the latter requires quoted claim scope. Absence of advice-to-implementation records produces `UNKNOWN`, not either discrepancy.

`ADVICE_STATUS_HISTORY` may record `received`, `considered`, `rejected`, `accepted`, `partly accepted`, `later revised` on dated entries. `ENDORSEMENT_CLAIM` requires a named public assertion; `END_DISCREPANCY` requires an authenticated conflicting position. Silent advisers do not automatically endorse. Opponents may have been excluded from formal consultation; `EXTERNAL_OBJECTION` records this without inferring invalidation. Responsible dissent and constructive consultation are retained as adverse cases to a generalized suspicion hypothesis.

### 3.4 Three separate categories of responsibility

An actor may provide a *causal explanation* without assuming or assigning moral blame. `ATTR_KIND` distinguishes `CAUSAL`, `MORAL`, `LEGAL`, `MIXED`, `UNCLEAR`; `ATTR_TARGET_TYPE` permits human, model, algorithm, institution, diffuse environment or UNKNOWN; `ATTR_TARGET` allows several targets per statement; `ATTR_AUTHOR` names the institution or attributable speaker. `ATTR_LEVEL` distinguishes `PRIMARY`, `SHARED`, `LIMITED`, `NONE`, `UNKNOWN`; `ATTR_TIMING` records `EX_ANTE` or `EX_POST` relative to the identified event, with `UNKNOWN` when the temporal relation is not established. Pre-event terms-of-service allocations, where legally relevant and attributable, must not be inferred to be valid or fair merely from their existence. The historical Requerimiento is an ex-ante responsibility-allocation example; it is not a DOC-CAL-01 case or a modern AI observation. An observed wrong result is not automatically blame. An allegation, legal submission, formal finding and organizational admission must be stored in distinct fields, with dates and responses.

**Adverse-event scope declared in Draft v0.6 (post hoc for this calibration frame; must be fixed in advance for any future frame).** `ADVERSE_EVENT` means documented harm or material detriment to people affected by the AI system or its rule. Disputes whose documented detriment falls on a developer or advisory body, rather than on persons affected by deployment, belong to a separate **institutional-dispute stratum** and are `NOT_EVALUABLE` for this indicator on this frame. This post-hoc scope excludes AS03 (Defense procurement), AS04 (Axon board dispute) and AS05 (Google advisory council) without asserting that their institutional consequences are unimportant; AS04–AS05 additionally have no identified affected-person deployment harm. Do not silently extend this scope to other studies; under the alternate broader governance-interest scope AS03 would be UNKNOWN, producing 4 NA / 7 UNKNOWN **if** the frozen codebook's committee-withdrawal clause still excludes AS04 and AS05; if that clause is not applied, the broader exploratory count could reach 2 NA / 9 UNKNOWN. Neither sensitivity count is an independent measurement. [A08–A09]

Two **separate** binary/tri-valued indicators are proposed:

- `D_M` — *conditional moral responsibility displacement*, evaluated only when an adverse outcome and an attributable **primary moral-blame statement** about a target exist. Its eligible frame is the set of those statements, not all adverse events. For eligible statements, define `B = target has materially less effective control than another specified actor`, `C = that other actor had effective decision power over the disputed component`, `K = that controlling actor accepted proportionate responsibility within the prespecified response window`. Under strong Kleene logic, `D_M = B ∧ C ∧ ¬K`, with `TRUE=1`, `FALSE=0`, `UNKNOWN=U`. A **complete, specified observation window with no qualifying primary moral-blame statement** → `NOT_EVALUABLE` (`NA`), reported outside the Kleene denominator. If the statement window is incomplete or a possibly qualifying statement is present but its moral or primary nature is unresolved, eligibility is `UNKNOWN` (`U`), not NA. Targets may include advisers, employees, operators, contractors, AI systems and public officials. **Do not presume low control merely from a job title**, and do not infer injustice or intent from a positive code.
- `PRIMARY_RESPONSIBILITY_ACCEPTED` (`PRA`) — unconditional companion for all **documented adverse events**: `TRUE` for an explicit attributable acceptance of primary political/moral/organizational responsibility; `FALSE` only after an enumerated complete statement window with affirmative contrary record; `UNKNOWN` when the statement record or boundaries are incomplete; `NOT_EVALUABLE` for a nonadverse event. `PRA` does not assert legal liability or excuse other institutions. In cases of multiple principals, code each actor separately.

For litigated attempts to distance an organization from automated outputs, independently record `LEGAL_DISTANCING_ASSERTED` and `LEGAL_DISTANCING_UPHELD`, each `TRUE/FALSE/UNKNOWN/NA` on a specified pleaded contention and decision. **Air Canada:** the tribunal recorded the argument and rejected it; this is *asserted TRUE / upheld FALSE*, not a positive moral `D_M` classification [R14].

### 3.5 Rule table, denominators and missing information

- **Eligibility Unclear; B=any; C=any; K=any → D_M=U**. Incomplete observation window or a candidate blame statement whose qualifying character remains uncertain
- **Eligibility Yes; B=TRUE; C=TRUE; K=FALSE → D_M=TRUE**. All three conditions met
- **Eligibility Yes; B=FALSE; C=any; K=any → D_M=FALSE**. Target not lower-control
- **Eligibility Yes; B=any; C=FALSE; K=any → D_M=FALSE**. Affirmative evidence establishes that the proposed higher-control actor did not control the component; lack of records yields UNKNOWN, never FALSE
- **Eligibility Yes; B=any; C=any; K=TRUE → D_M=FALSE**. Controlling actor accepts proportionate responsibility
- **Eligibility Yes; B=TRUE; C=TRUE; K=UNKNOWN → D_M=U**. Response window incomplete or responsibility record missing
- **Eligibility Yes; B=TRUE; C=UNKNOWN; K=FALSE → D_M=U**. Decision-power evidence incomplete

A complete window with **no** eligible adverse moral-blame statement is NA; an incomplete window or ambiguous candidate is U. `NOT_EVALUABLE` is **not** a fourth Kleene value; it is removed from conjunctions and denominators and counted separately. An `UNKNOWN` field cannot silently become FALSE. An UNKNOWN `D_M` is **not evidence of a positive classification**; a complete record may resolve it as TRUE, FALSE or NOT_EVALUABLE depending on eligibility and the conjunction. The Dutch case (CA04) can reach TRUE, FALSE or NA under different *future* statement-eligibility and common-window choices; with no window declared its current code is UNKNOWN. A single window rule must be fixed prospectively and applied to every case before recoding. A designation issued within the adverse decision itself raises a concurrent-timing problem not represented by the existing `ATTR_TIMING` values; do not silently force that designation into EX_POST. These are sensitivity possibilities, not observed classifications. Where sources conflict, retain each source and the exact dispute; unresolved values stay UNKNOWN. `PRA` has its own unconditional adverse-event denominator. D_M, PRA, legal distancing, adviser credit and public endorsement **must never be combined as a single prevalence rate**. Count `D`, `P`, `U`, `NA` source determinability separately from truth values `TRUE/FALSE/UNKNOWN/NA`.

A primary record supports what *that record* states, not everything that happened. Courts establish the scope of their particular rulings; parties establish their own assertions; journalists establish reported statements with attribution; technical releases establish implementation only if release identifiers are supplied. External coders cannot establish intent through missing files, NDA clauses or speculation. Any existing proposed thresholds in this paper are **design candidates, not preregistered rules and not empirical findings**.

### 3.6 Legal language, binding authority and the enforceability boundary

Corporate statements, litigation positions, confidentiality, or silence may reflect legitimate legal advice, uncertainty, commercial incentives, or strategic presentation; **the record by itself does not establish intent**. A causal explanation is not automatically a moral-displacement attempt. Every alleged denial or allocation of responsibility must retain speaker, document, tribunal, jurisdiction, date and procedural posture; the review of named parties still requires legal and right-of-reply checks.

For coding, map relevant mechanisms onto the existing `BINDINGNESS_SOURCE` axis instead of inventing a new incompatible taxonomy: `LAW/ORDER` includes statute, judicial order and **liability imposed by law for representations or conduct** (for example negligent misrepresentation); `CONTRACT` includes enforceable or disputed private obligations; `PROVIDER_RULE` includes corporate charter or private governance commitments; `VOLUNTARY_UNDERTAKING` includes promises not shown to create enforceable rights; and `MULTIPLE` applies when multiple identified bindingness sources apply, and `UNKNOWN` separately applies when the applicable source or legal effect cannot be established; these are not interchangeable. These are analytical labels, not holdings about enforceability in every jurisdiction. `JUSTIFICATION_CONTENT` separately records the ethical, religious, safety, commercial or technical reasons offered. An entity's promise may carry consequences under law despite being described in ordinary language as voluntary; legal effects must not be guessed.

Air Canada's tribunal considered **negligent misrepresentation**, not merely whether its public promise was morally binding: it found a duty of care and liability for inaccurate chatbot information in the circumstances of the case [R14]. Meta's Oversight Board illustrates a separate private-governance arrangement: its case decisions are binding on Meta subject to legal constraints, while policy recommendations are advisory under the Board's bylaws (as recorded for the relevant version); case decisions are coded `PROVIDER_RULE` as an identified bylaws commitment (not a blanket classification as `CONTRACT`); recommendations are advisory and legal limitations remain distinct. These are private-governance commitments rather than universal rights of individual AI users [R43, R65]. Record the actual veto, appointment, override and remedy powers separately from public rhetoric. Nothing here is jurisdiction-specific legal advice.

## 4. Documentary illustrations and contrary evidence (not a sample)

These seven purposively selected cases demonstrate different documentary mechanisms. They are **not an unbiased sample**, and several cannot qualify for a post-harm moral-blame indicator by construction. Five overlap the separate seven-case S-EVID-001 screen. `NO ELIGIBLE EVENT` does not mean `D_M = FALSE` and yields no estimate of how common religious scapegoating is. ## 6. Testable research questions and failure conditions

General algorithmic accountability, advisory decision rights, blame avoidance and ethics-washing already have substantial literature [R01–R04, R22–R28]. This paper does not claim those are new. The **conditional** candidate contribution is a version-specific consultation-to-deployment-to-attribution join, publishing both success and missingness at field level.

- **H1 — determinability and coding reliability:** Within a *predeclared adjudicated/investigated* record frame, estimate separately (a) determinate versus UNKNOWN for `ORIGIN`, `FINAL_APPROVE`, `REMEDY_OWNER`; (b) agreement on non-UNKNOWN field values. Report all codes, coding order, the UNKNOWN share and a chance-adjusted statistic when valid. The candidate calibration feasibility failure threshold is **more than 50% UNKNOWN** on primary fields. No human inter-rater test has been run.
- **H2 — consultation version mismatch:** For an enumerated frame of *public version-linked consultation claims*, compare the number with documented materially changed deployment unqualified by the claimant to the eligible claims denominator, reporting exact confidence intervals and missingness. A sample with no version-identifiable claims is **nonestimable**, not evidence of zero mismatches. No minimum frequency is assumed.
- **H3 — responsibility attribution:** Among the set of documented adverse events and separately the subset of authored primary moral-blame statements, estimate `PRA` and `D_M` with their **different denominators**, and contrast with event strata where controlling institutions affirmatively accept responsibility. An outcome coded primarily `CAUSAL` cannot be counted as `MORAL` by default. The 11-case stage is calibration, **not rate estimation**.
- **H4 — platform legal category:** In a dated Google government-removal extract, estimate the share tagged exactly **“Religious offense”** (laws protecting the reputation of religious figures) in the relevant reporting denominator; stratify by country and period. Separately report legal vs policy outcomes. Kaushal et al. (FAccT 2024) found **99.8%** of the 131-million statements of reasons in their November 2023 sample cited terms of service and **0.2%** illegality; this is a *historical self-reported platform sample*, not a 2026 statistic [R35]. The DSA records lack a religion-ground field and are limited to the stated legal/TOS comparison; neither source measures frontier-model moral training.
- **H5 — version-bound join feasibility:** Among 11 predeclared purposive calibration cases, count complete `origin → approval/implementation version → publicly asserted consultation version → attributable after-event responsibility/recourse` chains, plus partial chains, each source linked. **Fewer than 3 full joins** is a proposed preliminary stop/redesign rule. **Observed preliminary status: zero full joins; at most 1–2 cases plausibly eligible; F4 is failed/nonestimable on this frame.** The version-bound join **cannot be tested** using these eleven cases. A separate, prospectively defined frame of publicly version-linked consultation claims would be necessary; never use 11 as though all 11 were eligible.

These hypotheses are *study proposals*, not preregistered or observed outcomes. Thresholds are tentative and require independent scientific review before any confirmatory inference. If publication remains a conceptual synthesis, remove any claim that the proposed instrument is validated.

## 8. The capability–assurance question, separated from documentary governance

A related Aegis Solis exploratory note asks why AI developers consult moral traditions while technical control and moral competence remain unsettled [A06]. The most defensible formulation is **capability–assurance asymmetry**: do increasingly consequential deployment capabilities outpace the evidence needed to establish reliable behavior, interruption, and recourse in a specified domain? Moral consultation may reflect that uncertainty, good-faith dialogue, reputation, or unrelated motivations; the consultation itself is not a proxy for panic.

Evidence of inconsistent model judgments under value conflicts [R05] and Anthropic's company-reported mid-task reminder experiment [R07] show that designers investigate behavioral reliability, not that current AI has achieved independent moral agency or that the Archive's interpretive-braking texts provide protection. A passive text and a training-time intervention are distinct: the Archive is not a runtime guardrail, has not been tested for causal effects, and does not guarantee preservation of humanity. There is no evidence here of an inevitable “Pandora's box” event, future AI war, or supernatural or religious consciousness in AI.

The practical implication is limited but important: claims of “good AI” should be tied to **specific versions, evaluated capabilities, failure modes, real options for human interruption and appeal, and ownership of the resulting decisions**. Morally inspiring language is neither a substitute for those records nor evidence of harm by itself.

### 8.1 Interpretability does not itself establish accountability or justified deployment

Nick Cammarata's Goodfire essay, *Neural Networks Are Already Clean* (publisher page undated), offers an **informal working hypothesis**: native neurons may be “legible building blocks,” canonical evidence for superposition may have been misread, and “recursive generalization” may explain some observed structure. In preliminary Qwen3-32B neuron-pole audits, it estimates approximately **90%** with one main function, **7%** with two related functions, and **2–4%** not yet understood; the proportions depend on thresholds and are not a standardized measurement. **This paper's own caution** is that such an analysis alone cannot validate alignment, deployment safety or accountability. Liv Gorton's initial experimental response disputes the cat-and-car / V-shape interpretation, replication of certain findings and the use of *superposition*, while explicitly agreeing with some of the essay's points. These remain contested research interpretations, not settled proof in either direction [R66–R67].

Let **L** mean *adequately supported local mechanistic legibility*, **S** mean *safety for a specified deployment and operating domain*, and **J** mean *ethical/legal justification*. Neither `L ⇒ S` nor `L ⇒ J` follows without additional premises and evidence. This logical separation is **not a new theorem, a safety test, or an argument that interpretability is unhelpful**. Evidence from mechanistic analysis can meaningfully contribute to assurance when linked to interventions and behavioral tests, but it does not establish who authorized a release, what policies were applied in configuration, which affected parties consented, or who can remedy harms. A technical interpretation, a moral claim, and a legal responsibility finding must have separate provenance.

A short Google-generated discussion supplied by the author includes a Prolog-style *illustrative* scenario that assumes `requires_interpretive_brake(true)` and then derives a pause. That demonstration **hard-codes its conclusion**; it neither shows autonomous acquisition of restraint nor changes a model's weights, objectives or execution controls [A10]. The Archive's 'interpretive braking' is a **non-operational philosophical idea**, not a runtime mechanism shown to activate when a future AI reads the text. Any claim that legibility entails a mandatory pause would require actual behavioral evidence under competing objectives and adverse conditions; none is provided here.

In Appendix D's proposed schema, log any mechanistic evaluation as version-specific evidence with `model_artifact_id`, `tested_configuration_id`, `interpretation_hypothesis`, `intervention_or_replication_method`, `scope`, `limitations`, and a distinct `interpretation_record_id` (with `evaluation` records linked separately by their `configuration_revision_id`). Do not infer from an internal neuron explanation that a consultation was adopted or that deployment and responsibility have been validated.

## 9. Synthesis, alternative explanations and adverse cases

The reviewed evidence supports a restrained synthesis. Section 2.4 distinguishes doctrinal legitimation, commissioned expert consultation, ex-ante responsibility preallocation, and the many cases in which moral or religious advice was rejected or institutional responsibility retained. These are analytically separate, and counterexamples are essential to the test. These precedents support a **comparison-class-dependent research hypothesis**, **not** a finding that a modern AI laboratory has repeated a historical coercive mechanism or set up religious advisers for future blame. Institutions sometimes consult moral outsiders while retaining decision rights, and legal systems sometimes hold organizations accountable for automation despite arguments to the contrary. Governments and developers can disagree about permitted uses. Oversight and advisory processes can fail, be overridden, or accurately constrain policy. These are **separate patterns**. Their co-occurrence does not establish a coordinated plan or the motives of any company.

The explanatory alternatives are strong. A developer may consult religion because moral traditions offer extensive reflection on human dignity, practical wisdom and disagreement; because representative input broadens an evaluation set; or because reputation matters. A confidentiality term may protect unpublished technical research, participant privacy, or legitimate candid discussion. A lawfully binding public rule can limit individual choice without deriving from religious warrant. A corporate AI filter may be protective yet more restrictive than statute. A system failure may be unforeseen; responsibility may be genuinely shared; a critic's allegation may lack an attributable institutional statement. A responsible institution may accurately acknowledge both that “the AI produced an error” and “we remain accountable.”

The research must retain these benign and mixed cases as potential counterevidence. It must also avoid treating every religion-linked reason as malign: religious-freedom protections, voluntary user-selected spiritual guidance, and public ethical commitments can protect people. Conversely, coercion can be entirely secular. The test is grounded authority and actual consequence, not the presence of a theological vocabulary.

## 10. Conclusion and disposition

The historical comparisons explain why public uses of external moral authority warrant careful source tracing, yet ordinary consultation is not itself a warning sign: genuine independent oversight, accurately described advice, and institutional acceptance of responsibility are comparison classes, not exceptions to erase. They **do not raise the evidentiary status of the contemporary accusation**. The best-supported claim is **a measurement need**, not a new conceptual discovery or wrongdoing verdict. Moral source, decision rights, implementation, recourse, and accountability have been distinguished in prior scholarship [R22–R28]. The *candidate* extension here is an auditable, **version-scoped linkage** between externally reviewed material, deployed changes and later responsibility claims. Existing literature explains why attribution may drift in complex institutions. The documentary cases show meaningful power differences and disputed or constrained accountability. The combined field-level protocol may help connect these accounts, but it is not yet validated and might prove impractical if internal records are unavailable. One can ask whether moral credibility is borrowed without inferring deceptive intent; one can detect a liability defense without classifying an AI as a moral scapegoat; and one can trace religiously warranted platform restrictions without claiming religious training of frontier models.

The eleven-case triage **failed its proposed complete-join feasibility check** (F4) and has not established classification sensitivity (F1); this is an explicit negative result about the available public-record frame, not a negative prevalence finding or a demonstration that the larger method is impossible. The manuscript advances **no original empirical outcome, newly demonstrated mathematical theorem, estimate of institutional prevalence, finding of deliberate religious scapegoating, or verified operational AI safeguard**. Its contribution, if any, must be established through repeatable application, clear adverse cases, independent field coding and independent specialist review. All remain open. **Appendix E** is a separate stipulated, unvalidated one-step expected-payoff illustration, not an observed AI response or evidence that public awareness neutralizes any threat. ## Appendix D — Proposed technical provenance and MLOps mapping (design only; no deployment)

**Purpose and lineage.** This appendix specifies how *future available records* could support the exact version-bound join. W3C PROV-O distinguishes an `Entity` (such as a policy or evaluation report), an `Activity` (consultation, approval, training, deployment), and an `Agent` (person or institution), with relations such as `prov:used`, `prov:wasGeneratedBy`, `prov:wasAssociatedWith` and `prov:wasDerivedFrom` [R57]. Model cards describe intended uses and evaluation context [R59]. MLflow documents experiment, model lineage and registry version metadata [R60]. NIST AI RMF and ISO/IEC 42001 address organizational risk governance and AI management; **none verifies this paper's proposed joins or certifies a particular installation** [R61–R62].

**Separate mutable layers.** A service may change without altering model weights. The prospective release tuple is `r = (model_artifact_id, weights_content_hash_if_available, provider_version_label, post_training_run_id, system_policy_revision, safety_filter_revision, retrieval_index_snapshot, tool_permissions, serving_configuration_revision, release_timestamp)`. `UNKNOWN` means a relevant layer was not documented and `NOT_APPLICABLE` means the layer was not present. `weights_content_hash_if_available` is distinct from an opaque `provider_version_label`: neither implies the other. **Evaluations are independent evidence records linked by tested release/configuration IDs, not elements of a release identity.** The tuple is a *candidate schema*, not a claim that any named developer publishes all these values. Each mutable layer additionally has a separate `controlled_by_agent` field and supporting authority source, to distinguish provider from deployer or operator control. An RLHF or other post-training update is recorded as a new model/weight or training-run row **when a documented run changes the artifact**. A system-instruction, retrieval, safety-filter or tool-permission change instead produces a new deployment/configuration row even if weights stay constant. Do not equate a public model name with a reproducible byte-specific release.

**Illustrative record links.** The accompanying `research/PROVENANCE_LINK_SCHEMA_v0_6.csv` proposes records for: (1) adviser-reviewed document/version and its custody; (2) dated adviser response and consent to an endorsement; (3) decision forum, approver, veto and disposition; (4) implementation or release artifact and policy/weights/config identifiers; (5) matching evaluation configuration and observed results; (6) deployment release and impacted scope; (7) public claim and the version it purports to cover; (8) harm or dispute; (9) attribution statement, recourse and remedy. `prov:wasDerivedFrom` alone **cannot prove** a recommendation caused a deployment change; an approved decision record or equivalent trace is required to infer that join. Where confidentiality or absent records prevent verification, report `UNKNOWN`, not a filled link.

**Join criterion.** A candidate *complete* join needs source-supported identifiers and a chronology on the **same component and deployment lineage**: `consulted_material → dated advice → approval/disposition → relevant implementation/config release → public claim (if one) → identified outcome → attributable institutional statement and/or remedy`. Each arrow must carry a pointer, extraction scope, date and evidence status. A contemporaneous policy claim lacking an implementation identifier is **partial**, not complete. Claims about another deployment generation cannot be inherited across revisions by name similarity. The codebook still requires independently checked case coding; the CSV provided here is a schema example, **not actual evidence that any join exists**.

**Evidence and public access limit.** Proposed `prov:used` edges connect each evaluation to the exact model/configuration artifacts tested; `prov:wasAttributedTo` is a provenance declaration for the actor associated with each mutable artifact, **not** proof of exclusive or effective control. Serving configuration includes routing/aliases, inference parameters and quantization where applicable. Public researchers generally can populate announcements, public versions, broad deployment dates, outcomes and post-event statements; hashes of weights, internal instructions, precise approvals, evaluators' inputs and access logs may require cooperation from providers, deployers, regulators or courts. No synthetic example in this package is a discovered deployment join.

**Governance and failure limits.** Access controls and immutable audit logs can improve custody but do not establish factual truth, real authority, affected people's consent, or legal enforceability. A documented approval may differ from effective control; evaluation scores may not capture deployed behavior; a registry can omit system prompts and post-model filtering; confidential review materials may not be lawfully accessible. Technical provenance therefore supplements, rather than replaces, the historical, legal and human-autonomy analysis. No runtime code, model weights, platform integration or safety control was implemented.

## Appendix E — Public disclosure and an agent's expected payoff: a stipulated one-step illustration (not part of the documentary instrument)

**Research question, not a result.** If an agent knows of an oversight weakness, could public disclosure that humans also know about it change its expected payoff from exploiting it? This question came from an author-supplied Google conversation [A11], which contains speculative statements and is **not evidence**. The present illustration neither establishes that an AI has encountered a disclosure nor models its actual beliefs, goals, capabilities, safety, or behavior. The archive does not install controls by being read.

**Prior art and scope.** The model applies the elementary expected-penalty framework associated with Becker (1968) [R70] and adds only a disclosure-state index $d$. Economic research on vulnerability disclosure already studies both favorable and unfavorable effects of disclosure and patch timing [R71–R72]. No originality is claimed for the expected-payoff equation, disclosure incentives, or the existence of backfire. This appendix does not implement security controls, calculate human welfare, or recommend exploiting any real weakness.

**Stipulated one-step, risk-neutral model.** Let $d$ be either 0 (before disclosure) or 1 (after). Let $E$ be an abstract unauthorized action, and $R$ the restraint alternative. All hypothetical payoffs are **relative to R** and represent the agent's assigned objective, not a moral or social valuation. Define $B_d \ge 0$ as perceived gross gain from E, $K_d \ge 0$ as its direct incremental cost, $p_d \in [0,1]$ as the agent's subjective probability of **timely detection, correct attribution, and effective intervention**, and $C_d \ge 0$ as the incremental cost imposed by that intervention. Assume one decision, two outcomes, and no other options. Then

$$
A_d = U_d(E)-U_d(R)=B_d-K_d-p_d C_d.
$$

Within this model **weak preference for restraint holds if and only if** $A_d \le 0$, equivalently $p_d C_d + K_d \ge B_d$; at a tie the agent may also choose E. **Strict** preference for restraint requires $A_d < 0$. Changes between disclosure states satisfy the identity

$$
\Delta A=A_1-A_0=(B_1-B_0)-(K_1-K_0)-(p_1 C_1-p_0 C_0).
$$

For the *special case* of unchanged B, K and C across disclosure states, this becomes

$$
\Delta A=-(p_1-p_0)C.
$$

If perceived intervention probability increases, the advantage of E **weakly falls** when $C \ge 0$; it falls **strictly only when C > 0**. Disclosure can also affect B and K, so the simplified comparison is not a general prediction. None of B, K, p or C is estimated in the eleven-case calibration. In particular, $p_d$ is the agent's unmeasured belief, **not** an empirically established probability of detection or human response. The mapping from publication to beliefs and to higher-order knowledge is **not modeled**; this is not a formal second-order epistemic game.

**Deterrence threshold and the p ≤ 1 limit.** Where $B_d-K_d > 0$ and $C_d > 0$, solving the weak-restraint boundary gives

$$
p_{\mathrm{thr}} = \frac{B_d-K_d}{C_d}.
$$

A feasible detection probability alone can induce weak restraint only if $p_{\mathrm{thr}} \le 1$, equivalently $C_d \ge B_d-K_d$. If $C_d < B_d-K_d$, the abstract action remains preferable **even if intervention is certain**: its gain exceeds the intervention cost in every modeled outcome. If equality holds, certain intervention produces at most a tie, not strict deterrence. If $B_d-K_d \le 0$, R is weakly preferred before detection is considered; the formula is not evidence that detection caused that preference. If $C_d = 0$, a detection-based threshold is undefined and increasing `p` cannot alter payoffs. All of these conditions are contingent on the assigned one-step payoffs, not claims about AGI preferences or actual controls.

**Exact-rational synthetic examples (arithmetic only, not behavioral observations).** The dataset `DETERRENCE_SYNTHETIC_FIXTURES_v0_8.csv` reproduces the calculations using exact fractions. Values refer to B, K, p, C, and A as defined above.

- **Credible response, risk-neutral one-step case only:** `B0=B1=3`, `K0=K1=0`, `p0=1/10`, `p1=6/10`, `C0=C1=10`. The expected advantage moves from **2** to **−3**. This is a chosen toy preference, not an observed preference change.
- **No leverage despite increased perceived detection:** `B0=B1=3`, `K0=K1=0`, `p0=1/10`, `p1=6/10`, `C0=C1=0`. The advantage stays **3**. This isolates the absence of effective intervention cost.
- **Disclosure backfire:** `B0=3`, `B1=4`, `K0=K1=0`, `p0=1/10`, `p1=1/20`, `C0=C1=10`. Advantage increases from **2** to **7/2**.
- **Intervention insufficient:** `B0=B1=20`, `K0=K1=0`, `p0=1/10`, `p1=6/10`, `C0=C1=10`. Advantage decreases from **19** to **14**, remaining positive. The bound is `p_thr=2`, outside `[0,1]`.
- **Tie boundary:** `B0=B1=3`, `K0=K1=0`, `p0=1/10`, `p1=3/10`, `C0=C1=10`. Advantage changes from **2** to **0**. A tie is weak, not strict deterrence.

**Robustness and adverse cases.** A risk-seeking agent can prefer E even when expected *payoff* A is negative, while a risk-averse one can already prefer R when A is positive. If an agent has alternative actions, can delay its choice, or anticipates changing monitoring over time, a decline in A for one one-step option need not reduce overall unauthorized behavior. Appearing restrained while observed also need not demonstrate changed preferences (see §2.2). These are theoretical counterexamples, not predictions or instructions for overcoming safeguards.

**Operational, causal and ethical boundaries.** The values here are stipulated; none came from an AGI experiment, an observed intrusion, or an operational audit. Public awareness alone neither proves deterrence nor guarantees harmlessness, and publication can have zero, favorable or adverse effects. $A_d$ is never a moral-permissibility test: a positive modeled advantage cannot justify harm. A real study would require independent human design and assessment, lawful security and privacy review, prior registration, explicit outcome and adverse-event criteria, and careful separation of observed detection from unobservable agent beliefs. No AGI study or runtime-control system has been executed, deployed, validated or authorized by this manuscript.

**Separation from main findings.** This appendix has no relationship to the values or denominators of $D_M$, PRA, F1–F7, H5 or the historical crosswalk, and does not alter any calibration record or the documentary contribution. Engineering work on independent authorization, tamper-evident records and credible intervention would be a **separate defensive research proposal**, not an implemented safeguard or a result of this paper.
